Scope, timing, and useful terms

A kitchen project may include updates to cabinetry, counters, flooring, lighting, fixtures, or the room's layout. The exact work depends on what you want changed and what the existing space requires. Discuss the sequence with your contractor before work begins, including preparation, installation, and finishing. There is no single timeline for every kitchen; the scope, material choices, and any additional work uncovered during the project can affect how long it takes.

Key facts to clarify:
- What it covers: Ask which rooms, surfaces, fixtures, and finishing tasks are included.
- What affects cost: Scope, selected materials, layout changes, and extra preparation can all influence the overall cost. Request details rather than relying on a broad total alone.
- What affects timing: The amount of work, product availability, and changes to the plan may affect the schedule. Ask how the provider will communicate updates.
- Terms you may hear: “Scope” means the work included. “Allowance” is a planned amount for a selection that may not yet be chosen. “Change order” records an agreed adjustment to the work.

When comparing contractors, look for proposals that describe the same work in enough detail to compare. Ask what is excluded, how changes are handled, and who coordinates the different parts of the job. A lower total can be difficult to assess if preparation or finishing work is unclear.
